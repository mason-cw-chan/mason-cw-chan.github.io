# Introduction

I’m not going to present this in an academic way, because I want to share the idea more directly.

That was actually a thing happened about a month ago. My friend Astin posted this on Instagram: 

> Hi, my name is Astin. I believe a good amount of people know me, regardless of when or where our paths have crossed. Perhaps you should take part in this social experiment. The idea is to see who just skips through my stories and who pays attention. We are so focused on social media that we often forget the most important thing in life is relationships. If no one is reading this post, it will be a short experiment, but if you read this to the end, please send me a ‘hello’. I’ll then send this text to you with your name in it and have you publish it in your story. Don’t send me anything if you “don’t have time to post it.” It’ll ruin the purpose of this experiment. Let’s see who would be willing to expend their time to do it.

I found this immensely intriguing and immediately followed suit. While I was editing the copypasta, though, I noticed two issues.

1. Noticeability. As this story spreads across different people’s accounts, it creates a recognizable pattern. If viewers see the same block of text repeatedly, they may learn the “correct response” and reply automatically without actually reading. That undermines the original aim, which is to check whether people stop and read the story.
2. Forcibility. Once a viewer replies, they’re basically forced to participate. The line 'Don’t send me anything if you don’t have time to post it' works against the supposed goal. Someone might genuinely care but not want to repost (or just dislike chain posts), and this setup pushes them into an awkward choice: reply and feel obligated, or stay silent and look uncaring. Neither option helps the relationship.

Therefore, I made some modifications to the original copypasta and posted this to my story on 24 November 2025, 15:58: 

> Greetings, my name is Mason. I believe a good amount of people know me, regardless of when or where our paths have crossed. Perhaps you should take part in this social experiment. The idea is to see who just skips through my stories and who pays attention. We are so focused on social media that we often forget the most important thing in life is relationships. If no one is reading this post, it will be a short experiment, but if you read this to the end, please send me **(1)**. I’ll then send this text to you with your name in it and have you publish it in your story. If you want to display that you care for me, but don't have time to repost this or wish to opt out of the experiment, please reply me with **(2)**. Let’s see who would be willing to expend their time to do it.

Here, **(1)** and **(2)** were placeholders of instructions, which were different per person. For example, the one set of **(1)** and **(2)** that I used in my story were: 

**(1)**: the result of one plus one in the written form

**(2)**: the first word of the text

# Verdict

As implied from the title, this experiment comes out as a failure. The reason why I regard this as such is because it does not meet its aim, of which I will elaborate further below. 

# Reflection

Although improved, there are still some debatable points of this social experiment: 

## Homogeneity

The copypasta still follows the same structure: intro → instruction → more text → instruction. Aside from the placeholders, there isn’t much variation. When a viewer sees multiple stories of this kind, they will know what to respond before actually comprehend the full text as they can selectively read the instructions part. 

One friend of mine, Enoch, actually mentioned this. He said that he had seen many stories of this kind prior to seeing mine. This actually means the stories are overly homogeneous, which creates a highly observable pattern. 

Remember, the aim of this experiment is to test if viewers actually would bother to respond to stories to display genuine caring. However, if that care derives from merely reading a few words of a narrative without full understanding, that should not be caring, but rather robotic response. 

Yet, what if the prompts for people to repost are customized for everyone? There might also be two problems. First, the running cost of the experiment will be too high (at least the time cost is), as one needs to vary the prompt for every single participant (ie those willing to repost the copypasta), or at least creates a number of variations that are noticeably distinct but still follow the aim. 

This actually relates to the second problem. There will be deviations generated after multiple generations. As there is a possibility that people may overly change the prompts for the next generation, this amplifies the deviations from the original aim, which can give inaccurate results. 

## Validity

The original copypasta says: 

> The idea is to see who just skips through my stories and who pays attention. We are so focused on social media that we often forget the most important thing in life is relationships. 

This assumes that people who don’t pay attention to stories are attached to social media and therefore neglect real relationships. However, there is no causation between attachment to social media and detachment from real-life relationships, albeit there can be correlation between so. 

In fact, one can argue that people are fed up with social media, and choose to skim stories and posts. This situation actually means that there are potentially people who care for others and scroll away social media updates, which may lead to misjudgement on viewers' actions. 

Moreover, the following are also possible reasons that viewers do not respond to the story: 
- Busyness
- Dislike of chained content
- Missing of story by chance

It may be particularly perilous if the experiment conductors blindly determine that a particular person is not showing genuine care for them merely because of an inaction on social media. 

On the other hand, does it mean someone who is active on social media and reads every single story also uses that much effort to listen to others? I doubt if that is the case. 

## Insecurity

By this, I don’t mean the data collection is insecure, but rather the mentality behind running the experiment.  

When my friend Candies responded to me, they thought I was insecure. This is not the case for me, obviously, but can probably be the case for others if they are to conduct such an experiment. 

Their doubt is sensible. Ordinary people do not post stories which are totally different from what they used to post, not to mention a social experiment to check if viewers actually respond to them. When someone conducts this experiment, this can make their followers think that the conductor is insecure about their relationships. This can hamper their relationships, as the linkage built upon trust and loyalty has been shaken. 

Nontheless, this may be an economical way to check if friends are loyal and willing to show care. It usually takes a long period of time to determine if someone if loyal, and often one needs to have experienced different events to have known others' personalities. This is reflected in a Chinese saying: 知人口面不知心 ('you know people's faces but not their minds'). 

Yet, if it is cheap in time, it is to be expensive socially. 

# Conclusion

I am not really a big fan of social media, as I am too lazy to update. Yet, this social experiment on social media, although kind of failed, did provide new insights on the nature of social relationships.
